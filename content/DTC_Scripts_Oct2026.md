# DTC Brands You Need to Study: 9 Scripts (about 1:30 each)

Data: Euka Social Intelligence, US TikTok Shop, USD. Shop GMV covers 30 days (20 Sep to 19 Oct 2026). Video GMV covers videos posted since 1 Aug 2026.
Food & Beverage: 1–5. Beauty & Personal Care: 6–9.

## 1/10 RYZE Superfoods (F&B)
1/10 DTC brands you absolutely need to study: RYZE. They're doing about $1.4 million a month on TikTok Shop, up almost 200%, and the interesting part is how they sell mushroom coffee. They're not leading with mushrooms. They're leading with the morning. Look at their content: creators making their morning cup on camera, talking about coffee that doesn't upset their stomach, doesn't make them crash, no jitters. A big chunk of it is in Spanish, "café con hongos", with recipe videos and morning routines. And almost every top video pushes the same thing: the starter kit bundle, not a single bag. One creator generated over $48K in GMV from a single video, with 1.8 million views. And here's why that works. Nobody wakes up wanting adaptogenic mushrooms. They wake up wanting coffee that doesn't wreck their day. When you sell the feeling of the morning and put the bundle in front of them, the strange ingredient stops being a barrier and becomes a curiosity. Plus, a kit raises your average order to almost $57. The lesson? Sell the ritual people already have, and make the bundle the obvious way to start. Follow for more.

## 2/10 Bloom Nutrition (F&B)

Bulk packs plus limited-time flavors give the viewer both: it's cheaper, and it's something the shelf doesn't have yet. Every seasonal flavor drop restarts the cycle. The lesson? If shoppers already know your product, stop explaining it. Give them a reason to buy it on TikTok instead. Follow for more.

## 4/10 Ballerina Farm (F&B)
4/10 DTC brands you absolutely need to study: Ballerina Farm. They're doing over $1 million a month on TikTok Shop, and the interesting part is how they do it. They're not leading with creators going viral. They're leading with scarcity. Look at their content: "It's BACK. The bone broth hot chocolate just restocked after selling out TWICE." "The jar that broke the internet is finally here." "Stock up before it sells out again." Then the recipe content: a strawberry cheesecake yogurt bowl with 50 grams of protein, protein milkshakes, high-protein breakfasts, with one discount code repeated across dozens of small creators. Here's the wild part. Since August, no single creator video has cracked $3K in GMV. The top one did about $2,800. So the million comes from hundreds of small videos and a brand people already want. And here's why that works. When a product sells out, the restock becomes news. Every creator gets the same story to tell, "it's back, hurry", and one code makes it feel like a shared inside deal. The recipes then make a premium protein powder feel like part of a lifestyle, not a supplement. The lesson? You don't need one viral video. Build demand, run out on purpose, and make the restock the content. Follow for more.

## 6/10 BASED (Beauty & Personal Care)
6/10 DTC brands you absolutely need to study: BASED. They're doing over $5.2 million a month on TikTok Shop, and the interesting part is how they sell hair care. They're not leading with products. They're leading with kits by hair type. Look at their content: the Complete Curly Kit, the Deluxe Straight/Wavy Kit, the Ultimate Skin Set. Guys showing how to style a modern mullet, curly-hair glow-ups, "finally glowing up this school year", plenty of it in Spanish for the barbershop crowd. Each video is one problem, one hair type, one box. One creator with 16K followers had three videos in the brand's top eight, around $33K in GMV combined, and his best one did almost $13K on its own. And here's why that works. Most men don't know what a leave-in, a clay, and a sea salt spray do. Asking them to build a routine is friction. A kit labeled "curly" or "wavy" makes the decision for them, and the basket goes from one $15 item to a full set. Different hair types also mean different creators and different audiences for the same brand. The lesson? Don't make customers build their own routine. Package the answer by who they are. Follow for more.
